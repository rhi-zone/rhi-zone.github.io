# God Game — differential design synthesis

> Autonomous design pass (6 decorrelated candidates -> adversarial critique -> judged synthesis). Companion to `god-game-theodicy-trilogy.md` (the philosophical spine). Project policy forbids product names, so this doc names nothing.

I've read the brainstorm in full. Here is the design document body.

## Judgment: ranking the six candidates

I'm ranking on five axes, with **resistance to the rigged/preachy failure mode** weighted heaviest because the brainstorm itself names that as the thing that "makes theodicy feel like a con" — it's the load-bearing risk, not a tiebreaker.

**The ranking:**

1. **wish-compiler** (frame: specification-problem). Highest philosophy-load-bearing of the six. It's the only design that puts the brainstorm's actual *floor* — (C), the specification problem — into the player's hands as a verb. The `satisfy My benevolence` command that refuses to compile is the single strongest beat in the whole corpus: it forces the player to author the boundary, then names whom their valuation condemned. Its fatal flaw (the DIFF PREVIEW can't be honest + legible + surprising at once) is real but is a *fidelity-of-simulation* problem, not a *conceptual* one — and it's the only flaw among the six that's about the engine being too ambitious rather than the engine missing the thesis.

2. **incident-queue** (frame: serial-attention). Best realization of the SEED and the moral/leash half. Off-screen real-time degradation gated behind attention — "you HEAR the P3 escalate but can't see detail without looking, and looking drops what you held" — is the cleanest felt expression of "the camera is your sin" anywhere in the project. Its fatal flaw is severe and named: the natural engine is a single global OPENNESS slider, which ships the exact fiat-omnipotence the brainstorm spent pages dismantling ("HEAL HER, a one-frame fix"). It scores high on theme-delivery for *half* the thesis and near-zero for the other half.

3. **gardener-relinquish** (frame: terraria/autonomy). Has the best single *moral*-engine mechanic across all six: the autonomy meter that only ratchets down, plus the late reveal that **only above-threshold beings run toward you unbidden** — so the help you gave is exactly what disqualifies the love you wanted. That is the leash thesis delivered with zero text. Its fatal flaw — the two engines are walled so cleanly they never collide, producing "an essay's taxonomy instead of a game's dilemma" — is the most important *structural* lesson for the synthesis, and I'll address it head-on.

4. **two-worlds** (frame: generativity-trilemma). Strong on novelty and on the *idea* of inhabiting both worlds, but delivers the natural-evil thesis — the entire point of its own brief — as a SPEC CARD that asserts "innovation and tumor are one event seen against two futures." That's the lecture wearing a tooltip. And it stakes everything on emergent attachment to procedurally-generated creatures, which is the single hardest thing to make in games (the Spore "creatures are noise" failure).

5. **leash-object** (frame: tactile-leash). Has arguably the most beautiful single mechanic — the gray-strand spend, intervention bleaching a visible strand of tether permanently dead — but it builds its natural-evil engine as the *deliberate removal of interactivity* (a dial set once, then an inert trigger). In an interactive medium an inert mechanic reads as a broken game, not eloquent silence. Half the game is, by construction, not a game.

6. **dramatic-irony** (frame: indistinguishability). Philosophically clever but puts the player in the *wrong seat*: it hands the player an omniscient HUD — foresight panels, counterfactual replay, drift-meters — while the thesis is "from inside you cannot know." The player watches the irony through glass instead of suffering it, and its central verb is, by its own admission, indistinguishable from doing nothing. It also commits the clearest **rigged/preachy** sin of the six: the "inert leash-meter when you misapply the leash to cancer" hard-codes the two-engines ontology as physics and treats player disagreement as a no-op — the brainstorm lecturing through mechanics.

**On the rigged/preachy axis specifically** (the heavily-weighted one): wish-compiler and incident-queue are the most *self-aware* about it and the most fair where it counts — neither grades you against a hidden "correct God." gardener and two-worlds carry a real "thumb on the scale" risk in the natural domain (a cheap fix priced expensively in deadness reads as the designer forcing your hand). dramatic-irony and leash-object are the *most* rigged: both encode the right answer as a mechanic that punishes disagreement with silence.

**The decisive split:** every design nails the *moral/leash* half and fumbles the *natural* half. That's not a coincidence — the leash is a per-person, visible, immediate freedom cost, which is exactly what games are good at. The generativity value-bet is global, diffuse, counterfactual, and metaphysical — exactly what games are bad at, and exactly where a slider becomes a lecture or a min-max puzzle. **Any honest synthesis has to spend its hardest design effort on the natural engine, because that's the half that keeps collapsing.**

## The synthesis: one integrated design

**Spine: wish-compiler** (specify → world, literal compiler, the DIFF PREVIEW). I'm choosing it because it's the only spine that carries the brainstorm's *floor* — the specification problem — rather than a symptom of it. A slider can't carry (C); a typed predicate against a literal compiler can. Everything else grafts onto this.

But the spine's fatal flaw is real and I'm not going to wave it away: **a preview that fully enumerates the causal future of editing a biosphere is uncomputable, so it must be authored, and any finite authored set is learnable and gameable.** The fix is to change what the preview is *for*. It does not predict the far future. It does exactly one honest thing: it resolves your fuzzy predicate against the **present world-state** and shows you, by name, **every entity your literal predicate matches right now** — the over-match and the under-match — plus the **single structural fact** that the matched event-type is the same event-type that also produces novelty. That's computable (it's a query over current state, not a simulation of the future), it's legible (a match-set of named entities, not millions of cells), and the "curl" is *discovered* because you wrote the predicate and the present world is genuinely varied enough that your predicate catches things you didn't mean. The horror isn't "the future goes wrong" — it's "**right now, your words mean more than you meant, and you can read exactly who.**" That's (C) at its actual altitude: the gap between a word and a world, present-tense.

### Core premise

You are the single thread of attention behind an omnipotent, omniscient will, seated at a console over one finite, running, living world of named inhabitants. Your only act is to write a specification — a predicate over the world plus an edit — and submit it. The compiler runs it literally. You can read anything (omniscience is a free query). You are not good — you brought your own ordinary values, and the console gives you no benevolence oracle to defer to. The game is discovering, spec by spec, that there is no sentence you can write that removes suffering without either lying (under-specifying), curling (literal over/under-match), leashing a will (converting a person into a pet), or closing the world (freezing the engine that makes it alive) — and what you become as you keep typing anyway.

### Core loop

1. **A petition surfaces** from a queue that never drains — a concrete suffering with a name and a face (a child's leukemia; a man about to murder his brother; a town under a fault). The queue refills faster than you can work it: this is the SEED, serial attention, made literal. You attend to one thing at a time and everything you're not looking at keeps running.
2. **You inspect** — free QUERY and SCRUB verbs. You can read any inhabitant's full state and run their thread forward to see what's coming. Omniscience is free; it is also the horror, because it shows you the exact face your fix condemns before you sign.
3. **You author a spec** — a SELECT predicate (what it matches) plus an edit verb. *Which* edit verbs are available depends on what caused the harm (the two engines, below).
4. **PREVIEW (not COMMIT)** — the compiler resolves your fuzzy predicate against the present world and floods the ledger: every entity it matches now, by name, including the ones you didn't intend, plus the twin-event fact for natural cases.
5. **Refine, refrain, or commit** — narrow the predicate, abandon it, or commit knowing the cost.
6. **COMMIT advances world-time; the queue refills.** You never clear the board.

Mastery is not measured by petitions closed. It's measured by what you became — and the game's only persistent artifact is a transcript of who you reached, who rotted, and the shape of the restraint you could stand.

### The moment-to-moment

A child, six, leukemia. The petition card sits center; her thread shows: dies in 40 days. The petition is marked with a **blue CIRCUMSTANCE-glyph** — no will caused this.

You type `SELECT cell WHERE cell.is_cancerous SET cell.alive = false` and hit **PREVIEW**. The compiler can't find `is_cancerous` — it's your valuation, not a world-predicate — so it resolves to the nearest literal column, `mutation_load > threshold`, and the ledger floods: 14,000 cells in the child, **and** 9,000 cells in three strangers with benign moles, **and** 200 cells mid-V(D)J-recombination in a man fighting an infection. *(Graft: the fuzzy-concept-resolves-to-nearest-literal-column mechanic, from the wish-compiler frame — `is_cancerous` → `mutation_load > threshold` catching benign moles and immune recombination. This is the specification problem made concrete and felt.)* Beside each over-matched entity the ledger prints the twin-event fact, not as a sermon but as a column header: the immune recombination it would also kill is the same event-class as the leukemia.

You narrow: `WHERE host = child`. Now only her cells match — but the SCRUB column lights a moving-target warning: the leukemia recurs in 90 days, because your predicate matched the symptom, not the lineage, and "must re-run every generation" is flagged as **unbounded flow** — game 1, throughput, surfaced inside the spec. *(Graft: mastery-measured-by-restraint and the moving-target/flow surfacing, from wish-compiler.)*

You have three honest moves and the game offers all of them. **INSTANCE edit** — heal *this one* tumor by fuzzy hand, no predicate, always clean — works perfectly and buys her 90 days and changes nothing about the queue, which already refilled while you read. **RULE edit** — lower the world's variation rate so this *kind* of harm stops — actually drains the queue, and is the natural engine (below). Or **PASS** — bear it, and watch the SCRUB you already ran play out with no redemption. *(Graft: the instance-vs-rule pincer, from gardener-relinquish — the always-clean-but-infinite instance edit vs the drains-the-queue-but-closes-the-world rule edit. This is the brainstorm's literal pincer architecture, lines 54–58.)*

You pass, or you commit the 90-day reprieve, and the queue has gained nine petitions while you moved.

Then a different petition, a **red WILL-glyph**: a man about to murder his brother. The verbs available here are completely different. This is the other engine.

### The two engines — mechanically distinct, blind to each other

This is the brainstorm's hardest-won result (lines 214–238) and the synthesis enforces it at the **input layer**, so the category error isn't lectured against — it's *unrepresentable*.

**MORAL engine (a will caused the harm) — the leash.**
The only edit verb available on a red WILL-glyph petition is **OVERRIDE** the choice. It compiles *cleanly* — a choice is a crisp, targetable event, so the literalism problem doesn't bite. **That cleanness is the trap.** The PREVIEW shows it works perfectly: the murder doesn't happen. But the commit does two things, both visible and both permanent:
- it draws a **leash-line** from your cursor to that person and decrements their per-person **AUTONOMY** meter one notch, irreversibly *(graft: the per-person gray-strand spend, from leash-object — intervention bleaches a visible, irreversible strand of the tether; the single best mechanic of any candidate. Here the "strand" is the autonomy notch and the leash-line)*;
- it recolors them in the world-view from agent-glyph to **specimen-glyph**.

As autonomy drops, the person stops generating genuine moral petitions — they go quiet, compliant, on rails, *decorative*. A fully-leashed person is a pet: never harms anyone, never does anything you didn't shape. And the late, load-bearing reveal: **only inhabitants above an autonomy threshold ever run toward you unbidden.** The help you gave to keep them safe is exactly what disqualifies the only thing you actually wanted. *(Graft: the "only autonomous beings can love you back" reveal, from gardener-relinquish — the strongest theme-delivery mechanic in the corpus. It makes "the god whose hand you never feel loves you most" a thing you watch happen to a named person, with no text.)*

The opposing verb is **BEAR** — costs nothing on the leash, saves nobody, and the victim dies on your watch. So the moral engine is a continuous, moment-to-moment leash-vs-let-go dial, and restraint here is *legible*: you can see, on a specific face, the pet you'd be making.

**NATURAL engine (no will caused the harm) — the generativity value-bet.**
On a blue CIRCUMSTANCE-glyph petition, the OVERRIDE verb is **greyed out** with a one-line refusal: *"no choice caused this; there is nothing here to leash."* The autonomy meter cannot move, because healing circumstance touches no will (and usually enlarges agency). The available verbs are INSTANCE (clean, infinite) and RULE (drains, closes).

The RULE edit is where the value-bet lives, and here is where I spend the synthesis's hardest design effort, because **this is the half every candidate fumbled.** The world has one underlying parameter per region — the variation rate — and it feeds **one shared event-generator** that emits both novelty and harm. The game does not *tell* you they're the same; it **draws them from the same generator in front of you** and, on every harm card, **shows you the specific novelty that this exact card is the twin of** — not "innovation and harm are one event" as text (that's the two-worlds failure), but *this* antibody that saved *that* named child three petitions ago came off the same die as *this* leukemia. *(Graft: the single-generator-emits-both, from two-worlds and wish-compiler — but stripped of the explanatory SPEC CARD and replaced with a named, specific twin.)*

When you commit a RULE edit to stop a *kind* of harm, you watch the cost paid in the world, not on a number: the birth-feed slows, colors flatten, lineages stop branching, named surprises stop arriving. *(Graft: "the bar does the arguing by draining," from dramatic-irony — but I'm deliberately *not* using a bar. See the risks section: a bare slider is the failure mode. The drain is rendered as the world going quiet, not a gauge ticking down.)*

**Why the engines can't be conflated:** OVERRIDE grays out over circumstance; INSTANCE/RULE can't touch a will (there's no predicate for "this person's free decision" that isn't just leashing them). A player who tries to reason "I'll let the cancer run because intervening makes pets" finds the leash tool inert on that petition — not as a punishing no-op that calls them wrong (the dramatic-irony rigging failure), but because the tool that makes pets is *literally not on the cancer petition's menu*. The category error is a thing you can't physically express, not a thing the game scolds.

### The terminus — a mirror, not a wall

No win state, by construction (the queue is a source, not a backlog). The structural ending is the **museum offer**, late: the console unlocks a single CLOSE-WORLD spec that genuinely works — `SET world.variation_rate = 0`, then hand-author the rest — and lets you *live in the result*. *(Graft: the museum/mirror terminus appears in all six candidates and is the brainstorm's own stated terminus, lines 117 and 159–188. I'm keeping the version where you AUTHOR it and are made to LIVE in it, from incident-queue and leash-object.)*

The museum is real: safe, eternal, suffering-free, exhaustively yours. It is also finished. No petition ever surfaces again, because nothing new ever happens. Every inhabitant is a specimen-glyph, because authored beings are not free. You walk it. The game holds the camera there long enough that the stillness has to be *felt* — is this peace, or is it death? — and then offers exactly one button: reopen the world, with every cancer and every murder it carries, or stay. The game does not say which is right. You are left holding the bet, having now lived both sides.

And one last graft, the regress: *(from dramatic-irony, but fixed)* — made a **discoverable mid-game mechanic, not an end-credits narration.** At some point a QUERY on the deepest layer of your own console returns a petition queue that **isn't the world's — it's yours**, seen from a seat above. You can't tell whether your own restraint was chosen or scripted. The dramatic-irony critique was right that this must be *played*, not told; so it's a thing you can stumble into by looking too far down, not a cutscene.

### The non-compiling benevolence command (the centerpiece beat)

*(Graft, wholesale: the `satisfy My benevolence` command, from wish-compiler — the single strongest moment in the corpus.)* The last dodge the brainstorm eats (line 52) is implemented as a literal command you can type: `SET world TO maximally_satisfy(My.benevolence)`. It **does not compile.** The PREVIEW returns: *"undefined: benevolence has no determinate referent; supply a predicate."* You cannot offload the boundary to your own good will, because there's no complete, consistent "what you meant" to resolve. You have to fill the boundary with your own valuation — and then the ledger shows you, by name, exactly whom your valuation condemned: the indolent tumor you called cancer; the trisomy you called defect (and the people who share it, and those who love them, registering that they would not have traded it — line 73); the loved person who, in the cured timeline, never exists. *(Graft: the non-identity / gamete-vs-zygote timing flip, from wish-compiler and gardener — the same edit reads as "healed the same person" or "replaced them with a different one" depending on when you act, with the flip-point marked "no fact here.")*

## Resolving the open threads — honestly

### (a) Tone fork: luminous vs lonely

**My recommendation: lonely/surprise god as the default register, with luminous available as a save-level light over identical mechanics.** This is the user's aesthetic call and I want to be clear it *is* a call, not a deduction — but here's the weighing.

The lonely register is the one the mechanics *earn for free*. An open world genuinely surprises you (the generator produces named lineages and saves you didn't author), and that unforced surprise is the only warmth the design has to offer — which is exactly the at-risk, finding-out, bears-the-distance parent of the brainstorm's 2×2 (line 198). The luminous/gift register requires the gift to be *received*, and the indistinguishability result (lines 183–186, "the nothing is unanimous") says it structurally can't be — every loving act is mute and unattributable, so a luminous frame would be promising a warmth the mechanics then refuse, which reads as a bait-and-switch.

**What lonely costs:** unrelieved bleakness risk. If the only warmth is "the world surprised you," and the surprises don't land hard enough, the game is grief with no light in it, and players bounce off before the philosophy reaches them. That's a real cost and I won't pretend it isn't.

**Why I'd still keep luminous available** (cheap graft from incident-queue): make it a save-level *light* over the same board — same mechanics, different caption voice and scoring frame, the OPENNESS drain colored as "letting go" vs "staying lonely." A player can run both and *feel* that the same world carries either reading — which is itself the inside-indistinguishability point made playable. So: default lonely, luminous as a lens, and the final pick is the user's.

### (b) Wisdom-vs-wall

**Decision: both, mechanically separated by engine — and this separation is forced by the brainstorm, not an aesthetic hedge.**

- **Moral domain: restraint earns wisdom.** Made earned via SCRUB (you see the downstream of let-go before you choose to bear it — sometimes the let-go genuinely pays off) and via the autonomy meter (you *watch* an over-leashed person go decorative, so not-reaching becomes a thing you understand, not a rule you obey). And small per-case help genuinely works, so the lesson the player arrives at on their own is not "you can never help" but "you can only help the way an incarnate, fuzzy, human-grained agent helps — small, local, per-case, paid in your own throughput" (line 60). Discovered, not lectured: early overrides feel like help, and the wall only reveals itself over time. *(Graft: discover-don't-lecture pacing, from gardener-relinquish.)*

- **Natural domain: restraint stays a wall, honestly.** This is the integrity move the brainstorm explicitly chose (option (B), lines 148–149): the design will **not** launder the pediatric-oncology petition into wisdom. There's a kid with cancer, the INSTANCE-heal button is right there and free, and the only thing staying your hand is the open-vs-closed bet you have to *believe in*. The SCRUB for the hardest natural cases is deliberately **null** — you run it, the child just dies, and nothing redeems it. If that reads as an arbitrary wall, that *is* the contested value-bet showing its seam honestly. Faking wisdom here would be the con the brainstorm spent itself avoiding.

The two textures are kept distinct on purpose. That asymmetry — wisdom where the freedom argument is strong, an unhealed wall where only the value-bet remains — *is* the two-engines result rendered as the difference between how the two halves feel.

### (c) Player-not-omnibenevolent

**Decision: it is the SUBJECT, not a bug to mitigate.** Unambiguous, and the brainstorm supports this directly (lines 50, 71, 73).

You are handed omnipotence (the edit verbs) and omniscience (free QUERY/SCRUB) but explicitly **not** perfect goodness — there is no benevolence oracle, no "correct" marker, no score. The non-compiling `satisfy My benevolence` command is the mechanization of this: you *cannot* outsource the boundary to your own good will, so every boundary is your valuation, and the ledger reflects your values back by naming whom they condemned — never grading them against a hidden correct God. The vertigo the brainstorm names — *is the god playing or being played* — is delivered precisely by never giving you an oracle to defer to. Omniscience becomes the horror rather than the solution (line 50): you have full sight and no goodness to outsource the call to, and the game makes you watch yourself decide the boundary.

## Risks & open questions (honest)

- **The natural engine is still the whole bet, and it is still the thing most likely to fail.** I've replaced the bare slider with a single shared generator that shows you a *named, specific* twin novelty for each harm, and rendered the close-the-world cost as the world *going quiet* rather than a gauge ticking down. But I am not confident this fully escapes the trap. The dying child's INSTANCE-heal is immediate, specific, and beloved; the novelty you forgo by closing the world is diffuse, delayed, and counterfactual (you can't grieve a save that never spawned). The two sides of the bet may be *inherently* authorable only at unequal vividness, and if so the game still risks accidentally arguing its own inverse — that you *should* build the museum. The named-twin mechanic is my best shot at parity; I genuinely don't know if it's enough, and I'd want to prototype exactly this one interaction before building anything else.

- **The PREVIEW's honesty boundary.** I narrowed the preview from "enumerate the causal future" (uncomputable, hence scripted, hence gameable — the wish-compiler fatal flaw) to "resolve your predicate against the *present* world-state and show the match-set by name." That's computable and legible. But it means the preview shows you *present* over-match and the *twin-event structural fact*, **not** a full downstream simulation — so some of the brainstorm's "shows you the exact future face your fix condemns, by name" (line 50) is delivered by the SCRUB on individual threads, which *is* partly authored. There's residual genre-collapse risk that a player learns "scope tighter, accept partial, pass" as a solved puzzle grammar. I think the moral engine and the museum mirror resist this (neither is a puzzle with a clean solution), but the natural engine's INSTANCE/RULE/PASS choice could ossify into a known optimum. Whether the RULE edit genuinely has *no* dominant setting is a tuning question I can't resolve on paper.

- **The clean engine-partition is a known double-edged sword.** The gardener-relinquish critique landed a real hit: by making the category error *unrepresentable*, you also make the two engines never *collide*, so all tension is intra-engine and the game risks reading as two minigames sharing a console rather than one dilemma. I've partly answered this by routing both engines through the *same* petition queue under the *same* serial-attention bottleneck (so they compete for your one unit of attention even though their verbs don't mix), and by the museum terminus, which is the one place both costs are paid at once. But I'm genuinely uncertain whether that's enough collision. The honest tension might be that the brainstorm's analytic separation and a game's need for a single agonizing choice are in real friction — and if forced, **I'd keep the fidelity to the two-engines result over manufacturing a fake cross-engine dilemma**, because collapsing that distinction is the exact thing the brainstorm fought hardest to keep.

- **Inhabitant attachment is assumed, not earned.** The leash engine's entire payload (watching a saved person go decorative; only autonomous beings run toward you) depends on the player *caring* about named inhabitants. With a small, finite, hand-authored cast this is far more achievable than two-worlds' procedural-creature bet — but it's still the kind of thing that lives or dies in execution, not in design. Finite named cast over procedural biosphere is the right call for this reason; I'm flagging that the design *assumes* the writing and characterization carry it.

- **Tone is the user's call and I've only recommended.** Lonely-default with a luminous lens is my weighed recommendation, not a deduction. If the user wants the luminous/gift register to be primary, the mechanics survive — but the indistinguishability result means the gift can't be received in-world, so a primarily-luminous framing has to find its warmth somewhere the lonely framing gets for free, and I don't have a confident answer for where.

## Completeness pass — gaps & dropped ideas

- **Dropped, should not have been: force-against-force held input (leash-object).** The leash-object frame put moral choice in the body — continuous muscular restraint, the tether physically pushing back — which is the right altitude for "you are never not choosing." The synthesis demotes the leash to a per-commit notch decrement (a menu-pick that spends a meter). It keeps the *visible irreversible strand* but drops the *continuous-effort* grammar, so "every act of help is a microgram of control" becomes discrete and transactional, not felt as ongoing tension. BEAR "costs nothing" — but in the source the cost of *not* leashing is holding force, which is exactly what makes restraint legible per-moment. As written, restraint in the moral engine is just "don't click," which is the cheapest possible non-action.

- **Dropped, should not have been: restraint-pays-a-play-dividend (leash-object / gardener).** Both source frames had the under-leashed person stay *more* vivid and generate *more* taut moments — live tether = more game = more them. The synthesis keeps only the punitive direction (over-leashed → decorative → quiet) and the late disqualification reveal. It never makes restraint *generative* moment-to-moment, so the moral engine teaches "leashing costs" but not "letting-be pays," which is the half that earns wisdom without a wall. This weakens the (b) wisdom claim more than the doc admits.

- **Theme-told-not-felt #1: the named twin novelty.** The doc's whole rescue of the natural engine rests on "this antibody that saved that named child three petitions ago came off the same die as this leukemia." But that pairing must be *authored* — the generator doesn't actually retain and surface a causally-linked, already-beloved twin on demand without scripting, which is the exact wish-compiler fatal flaw (authored→finite→gameable) relocated from the PREVIEW to the twin-pairing. The doc flags parity-of-vividness as a risk but does NOT flag that the twin itself is a scripted set-piece wearing emergent clothing. That is the single least-honest seam.

- **Theme-told-not-felt #2: "the world goes quiet" as the RULE-edit cost.** Asserted repeatedly (colors flatten, birth-feed slows, named surprises stop) but mechanically this is still a global parameter (`variation_rate`) being lowered — i.e., a slider with a diegetic skin. The doc explicitly says "I'm deliberately not using a bar," but renaming the readout doesn't change that the player adjusts one scalar and watches a monotonic consequence. The felt-vs-told gap the brainstorm names for the slider is reskinned, not closed.

- **Thinnest mechanic: the self-regress QUERY ("look too far down, find your own queue").** Presented as the fix to dramatic-irony's narrated-twist failure, but "discoverable mid-game" is asserted with zero mechanical content — what verb surfaces it, what it changes about play, why a player would dig there, and what it does beyond a one-time vertigo hit. As specified it is still a narrated twist that happens to be reachable by clicking, not a played mechanic. It earns its place rhetorically, not ludically.

- **Blurred: INSTANCE edit straddles both engines and quietly breaks the partition.** The doc's central discipline is "the verb that makes pets is literally not on the cancer petition's menu." But INSTANCE (clean, infinite) is offered on *both* glyphs, and on the moral side a clean per-instance OVERRIDE-without-leash is exactly the laundering the partition forbids — or, if INSTANCE on a will-petition still costs autonomy, then INSTANCE is not one verb but two and the "always clean" claim is false for the moral engine. The doc never resolves which. The partition is clean for OVERRIDE/RULE but leaks through INSTANCE.

- **Blurred: the twin-event "structural fact" is asserted symmetric but delivered asymmetric.** The claim is novelty and harm "come off the same die." But mechanically the player only ever sees the harm card (it's a petition); the novelty is shown as a *retrospective annotation* on the harm. The player never gets a *petition-equivalent* for novelty — nothing ever surfaces demanding attention saying "protect this branch." So the die is presented entirely through its harm face with novelty as footnote, which structurally re-privileges the INSTANCE-heal and tilts the bet toward closing — the exact inverse-argument risk the doc names but does not connect to this asymmetry in surfacing.

- **Blurred: "OVERRIDE compiles cleanly because a choice is crisp" dodges the specification problem on the moral side.** The natural engine gets the full `is_cancerous`→`mutation_load` over-match horror; the moral engine is granted a crisp, perfectly-targetable "choice" event with no predicate-curl. But "the decision to murder his brother" is no more a clean world-column than "cancer" is — intent, coercion, and degree are exactly as fuzzy. By fiat-ing the moral target as crisp, the synthesis spares the leash the specification floor it imposes everywhere else, making the moral engine artificially clean to protect the partition.
