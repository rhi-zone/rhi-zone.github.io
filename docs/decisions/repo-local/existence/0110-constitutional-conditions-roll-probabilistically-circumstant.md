# ADR-0110: Constitutional conditions roll probabilistically; circumstantial conditions derive deterministically from life history

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-05-29

**Context.** Health conditions need a chargen origin. The uniform easy approach is a probabilistic random roll for every condition.

**Decision.** Split conditions by origin. Constitutional (genetic) conditions get a probabilistic chargen roll grounded in real prevalence data. Circumstantial conditions (dental disease, chronic pain from injury, diabetes) are derived deterministically from generated life history. A random roll for a circumstantial condition is the wrong model, not a crude version of the right one; when the upstream history isn't built yet, leave the condition unassigned and document the missing systems.

**Alternatives rejected.**
- *Use a probabilistic random roll for all conditions, including circumstantial ones* — A circumstantial condition has a cause in the character's history; rolling for it randomly is categorically the wrong model rather than a low-fidelity approximation, so it is rejected even as a stopgap — better to leave unassigned and document the needed upstream systems.

**Consequences.** Chargen for circumstantial conditions blocks on the backstory/history systems (e.g. dental chargen is specified but not yet wired to economic_origin). This is one instance of the broader 'characters have histories' direction: every chargen roll not derived from simulated history is a documented debt to be replaced.
